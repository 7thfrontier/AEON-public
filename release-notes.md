Turn an escape-time fractal in the frame · 2026-11-01

- **Animated exports keep their frame timing.** A frame that was slow to render, or an export left running in a background tab, could stay on screen longer than one frame in the saved video, so motion stuttered: frames landed 51 to 133 ms apart at 24 fps, where 42 is right. Where the browser can encode VP9 or VP8 through WebCodecs, as Chrome, Edge and the Munder Difflin app do, Frame-Exact Export and Record Video now stamp every frame at its exact time and write the WebM themselves, with no added library. An MP4 recording, and a browser without that encoder, keep the previous recorder.
- **Move, Scale and Rotate work on a single layer.** With one layer in Layer Studio, turning it (a Buddhabrot or any other type) stored the angle and left the picture as it was, because a layer's move, scale and turn were drawn only by Live Composite with two or more visible layers. A lone layer you move, scale or turn now shows that way on screen and in its exports, with Live Composite on or off, and Move on canvas opens with one layer.
- **Variation sheet cells keep sharpening while you press keys.** Since 7.7.14, in a variation sheet of attractors, IFS fractals, flames or Buddhabrots, refinement could stall for as long as you kept pressing keys: each key restarted the active cell's transparent picture, and the other cells waited behind it at their quick preview. An interrupted picture now waits for the other cells, which sharpen between keys, and lands after them.
- **Turn an escape-time fractal in the frame.** A View Rotation slider under Navigate turns the picture about its center, so a form can lie at any angle in a wide or tall composition instead of only the angle its formula gives it. Panning, zooming, the Julia picker, orbit tracing, exports, share links, view history and motion keyframes all follow the turn. WebGPU and GPU deep zoom do not turn yet, so a turned view renders on WebGL2 or the CPU, and the WebGPU badge names View Rotation as the reason.
